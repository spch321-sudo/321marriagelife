0	Fruit of the Spirit (6): Goodness
1	A Secret-Angel Weekend
2	At the start of the weekend, each of you quietly decide on one good thing you will do for the other in secret—don’t say what it is yet.
3	Over the weekend, quietly do it: perhaps polish their shoes, mend a piece of clothing, or tidy their desk.
4	Before the weekend ends, have a cup of tea together. Take turns guessing what the other did for you, then share how it felt to be treated with such goodness.
5	Read Romans 12:10 together, and say to each other: “Thank you for honoring me and putting me first.” Then pray together for your home.
6	Goodness Comes from God
7	Once we have tasted God’s goodness, we can bring goodness home
8	Be still and recall the last time you thought, “God is so good”—maybe just a hot meal or a good night’s sleep. Tell the Lord: “Lord, you are good. Today I want to taste your goodness first, then bring it to my family.”
9	321 Light: Goodness from the self-ruled life is a transaction: “I’m good to you so you’ll be good to me.” As soon as there’s no response, goodwill turns into resentment. Goodness from the self-emptied life is received from God and then freely given; the focus is on Jesus, not on whether my spouse pays me back.
10	The book of Acts describes Jesus as one “who went about doing good” (Acts 10:38). His goodness was not a slogan but something he did all along the way: touching lepers, feeding the hungry, giving a weeping widow back her son. Jesus shows us that goodness is God’s character put into action.
11	Good Father, thank you for treating us well every day with so much grace, seen and unseen. We confess that our goodness is often limited and mixed with calculation. Let us taste your goodness daily and be filled with it, so that at home we freely give away your goodness. In the name of the Lord Jesus, Amen.
12	You only know whether an apple is sweet by biting into it; looking at a picture isn’t enough. The psalmist says, “Oh taste and see that the LORD is good” (Psalm 34:8). He isn’t inviting us to study God’s goodness but to taste it for ourselves. The fruit of goodness doesn’t start with trying hard to be a good person; it starts with experiencing how good God is.
13	A man ran up and called Jesus “Good Teacher,” but Jesus answered, “Why do you call me good? No one is good except one—God” (Mark 10:18). Jesus wasn’t denying his own goodness; he was turning the man’s eyes back to the source: true goodness is found only in God. Our own goodness is often mixed with the calculation, “I’ve done so much—you ought to be good to me too.” God’s goodness is pure and asks nothing in return.
14	This is great news for marriage. After years together, it can feel as if our “supply of goodness” has run dry and we can’t squeeze out one more pleasant look. But goodness is fruit the Spirit bears, not something we squeeze out of ourselves. When we return to God each day to taste his goodness, those who have been treated well by him will naturally treat well those around them. A changed life changes how we live.
15	When was the last time I personally “tasted” God’s goodness?
16	How much of my kindness toward my spouse carries the calculation “you ought to be good to me too”?
17	Share a small thing this week that made you think, “God is so good.”
18	What have I done that made you feel I was truly good to you? How did you feel at that moment?
19	Write down three small things today that made you think, “God is so good”
20	Tell your spouse: “Thank you. Today you were so good to me when you...”
21	Do one small thing for your spouse without expecting anything in return
22	“Oh taste and see that the LORD is good” — Lord, right now let me taste your goodness, so that I can treat those around me well.
23	Tender Affection, Outdoing One Another in Honor
24	Goodness is love without pretense that races to put the other first
25	Place your hand on your chest and be still for a moment. Ask the Lord: “Lord, is there anywhere my love for my spouse is only on the surface, only an act?” You don’t need to answer right away; let the Holy Spirit gently shine his light on you.
26	321 Light: At home, the proud compete for position: who’s right, who should give in first, who works harder. The humble compete for opportunities: who can honor first, who can serve first. Pride provokes pride, and both lose; yielding inspires yielding, and both win.
27	Jesus said, “I am among you as one who serves” (Luke 22:27). While the disciples were arguing about who was the greatest, Jesus picked up a towel and washed their feet himself. He didn’t wait for them to honor him first; he bent down first. This is the most beautiful picture of “in honor prefer one another.”
28	Lord Jesus, you are the King of kings, yet you are among us as one who serves. Remove the pretense from our love, and make us tenderly affectionate at home, outdoing one another in honor. Help us stop arguing about who’s right and who’s wrong, and instead race to honor and to serve, so that our home is filled with real warmth. Amen.
29	When Paul describes a life of goodness, his first line is: “Let love be without hypocrisy” (Romans 12:9). The Greek word for hypocrisy originally described an actor on stage wearing a mask. Marriage has masks too: loving in front of others, cold at home; saying “fine” while keeping score inside. The first step of goodness is to take off the mask and let love be real.
30	Paul goes on: “In love of the brothers be tenderly affectionate to one another; in honor prefer one another” (Romans 12:10). “Tenderly affectionate” describes the natural warmth and affection of family. “In honor prefer one another” carries the sense of leading the way in showing honor—racing to respect the other first. Picture a door with two people each hurrying to hold it open so the other can go first. That’s a picture of a good home: not arguing over who deserves respect first, but competing to see who can show respect first.
31	The goodness in this passage is very concrete: “not lagging in diligence” and “given to hospitality” (Romans 12:11, 13). At home, that means not pushing all the chores onto the other person, stepping in when they’re tired, and welcoming our spouse as the most honored guest in our house. Goodness is not a feeling; it’s a string of good deeds we race each other to do.
32	In our marriage, do I more often compete for position or for opportunities?
33	Is there a setting where my attitude toward my spouse in public is very different from at home?
34	If I treated you as the most honored guest in our home, how would you most like me to welcome you?
35	Lately, in what way do you feel I’ve put you first and made you feel respected?
36	Today, beat your spouse to a household chore they usually handle
37	At mealtime, serve your spouse the best portion first
38	Thank your spouse in the tone you would use with an honored guest
39	“in honor prefer one another” — Lord, right now let me race to honor others rather than compete for position.
40	Created for Good Works
41	God has prepared good works for us to walk in at home
42	Quietly think through the places you will pass through at home today: the kitchen, the dinner table, the bedside. Tell the Lord: “Lord, you have already prepared good works for me in these places. Open my eyes to see them.”
43	321 Light: Change your mindset and your life will change. If we see chores as “my burden,” we feel more and more hard done by; if we see them as “good works God prepared for me,” they become more and more meaningful. When we shift from a human point of view to God’s, the same small task takes on eternal value.
44	Passing under Zacchaeus’s tree, Jesus stopped and said, “Zacchaeus, hurry and come down, for today I must stay at your house” (Luke 19:5). In a man everyone despised, he saw a good work God had prepared. Jesus shows us that good people have eyes that see opportunities—they stop where others walk on by.
45	God our Creator, thank you for re-creating us in Christ as your workmanship, and for preparing good works for us to do. Open our eyes to the small opportunities in our home. When we grow weary in doing good, strengthen us so we don’t lose heart or give up, for in due season we will reap. Amen.
46	Paul says, “For we are his workmanship, created in Christ Jesus for good works” (Ephesians 2:10). The word “workmanship” carries the sense of a work of art, a masterpiece. God has re-created us as his masterpiece, and one purpose is good works. Just as a fine violin is made to play beautiful music, a person renewed by Christ is made to live a beautiful life.
47	Even more wonderful, these are good works “which God prepared before that we would walk in them” (Ephesians 2:10). God hasn’t handed us a heavy to-do list. Like a loving father, he has set gifts along the path ahead of time, waiting for us to pick them up. Maybe it’s a cup of hot tea when your spouse gets home from work, maybe a word of encouragement when they’re feeling low. Goodness is often not about finding big things to do, but seeing the small things God has already placed in front of us.
48	But doing good can wear us out, especially at home, where our efforts often go unseen. So Paul encourages us: “Let’s not be weary in doing good, for we will reap in due season if we don’t give up” (Galatians 6:9). He adds, “especially toward those who are of the household of the faith” (Galatians 6:10)—and that surely begins with the household we share. We often save our best goodwill for outsiders and leave our most worn-out selves for family. The practice of goodness has to start with the one closest to us.
49	What good work might God have prepared in our home that I’ve been missing?
50	Do I give my best goodwill to outsiders and leave my most worn-out self for my spouse?
51	When are you most tired at home lately, and most in need of a helping hand?
52	Let’s think together: what good deed could we do together this month for family or neighbors?
53	Today, watch for one small good work God has prepared, and do it
54	At the time your spouse is most tired, step in and take over a task
55	Save your gentlest tone for the first words you say when you get home today
56	“Let’s not be weary in doing good” — Lord, right now, tired as I am, I still want to do good to my family.
57	Doing Good Where No One Sees
58	The Father sees the good we do in secret
59	Imagine the Father gently watching you as you do housework alone, when no one notices. Quietly tell him: “Father, thank you for seeing what I give in secret. Today I want to do it for you alone.”
60	321 Light: The self-ruled life needs an audience to do good; if no one sees, it doesn’t seem worth it. The self-emptied life needs only one audience: God. To let Jesus receive all the glory means finishing the task without signing our name to it.
61	After healing a man with leprosy, Jesus told him, “See that you say nothing to anybody” (Mark 1:44). He did many miracles yet often stepped aside, not seeking people’s praise. His greatest act of goodness was completed on the cross, where onlookers saw only shame, not glory. Jesus’ goodness never needed applause.
62	Father who sees in secret, thank you for seeing every unnoticed thing we give. Purify our motives for doing good, so that we no longer need applause or keep a tab. Let us love and serve quietly where the other cannot see, so that our home is like a deep-rooted tree, steady and blessed. Amen.
63	The most important part of a great tree is its roots, buried underground where no one sees them. Marriage is the same. What really makes a home steady is often not the big anniversary surprise, but countless unseen acts of goodness: pulling the covers over your spouse in the middle of the night, quietly saving the food they love, speaking well of them in front of others.
64	Jesus said, “don’t let your left hand know what your right hand does” (Matthew 6:3). It’s a vivid way of saying: when you do good, you don’t even need to keep remembering it yourself. In marriage, what hurts most is not failing to do things, but doing them and then constantly reminding: “Look how much I’ve done for you!” Then the good deed becomes an IOU.
65	Jesus also said, “your Father who sees in secret will reward you openly” (Matthew 6:4). We can rest assured that no small thing done out of love is wasted; the Father sees it all. When we no longer need our spouse’s applause, we are truly free to love. And wonderfully, hidden goodness is often the most moving, because it carries the fragrance of the Father.
66	Among the things I do for my spouse, which ones have I been quietly “keeping a tab” on?
67	If no one saw, which good things would I still be willing to keep doing?
68	Have you ever discovered something I did for you in secret? How did it make you feel?
69	Is there something you’ve been doing quietly that you think I may not have noticed? Let me thank you today.
70	Today, do something for your spouse in secret, and don’t mention it
71	Thank your spouse for something they’ve been quietly doing all along
72	Tear up one inner IOU of “what I’ve done for you,” and hand it to God
73	“your Father who sees in secret will reward you openly” — Father, right now I do good for you alone; I don’t need applause.
74	Overcome Evil with Good
75	When we are wronged, goodness is the overcoming response
76	Recall the last time you felt wronged or misunderstood at home. Don’t rush to defend yourself. Gently place that hurt in the Lord’s hands and say: “Lord, teach me the way to overcome.”
77	321 Light: Repaying evil for evil is the self on the throne: “You hurt me, so I’ll get even.” Overcoming evil with good is letting Jesus reign: “Vengeance belongs to the Lord; my part is to do good.” Laying down the right to get even and returning to our rightful place is humility, and humility inspires humility. That is often where revival in a family begins.
78	Nailed to the cross, enduring the greatest injustice, Jesus said, “Father, forgive them, for they don’t know what they are doing” (Luke 23:34). He did not repay evil for evil but overcame sin with love and forgiveness. The cross is the greatest victory of good over evil.
79	Lord Jesus, on the cross you overcame evil with good, and you overcame our sin too. Forgive us for the times we have repaid evil for evil and refused to give way. When we are wronged, help us leave justice in your hands and respond with goodness. And protect everyone in our home, so that our home becomes a safe, warm place where we treat each other well. Amen.
80	The most common cycle between spouses goes like this: you go cold, so I go cold; you get loud, so I get louder. Paul says, “Repay no one evil for evil” (Romans 12:17). Repaying evil for evil is like two people throwing mud at each other: in the end both are filthy and the house is a mess. Goodness is the first one to put the mud down.
81	Paul concludes: “Don’t be overcome by evil, but overcome evil with good” (Romans 12:21). Repaying evil for evil looks like fighting back, but in fact it means evil has beaten me, because I’ve let the other person’s wrong decide my response. Overcoming evil with good means letting the Holy Spirit, not the other person’s emotions, decide how I respond. When my spouse says something cutting, I can choose not to cut back, and say calmly: “That made me a bit sad. Can we talk this through?”
82	But let’s be clear: overcoming evil with good does not mean tolerating harm. Paul says, “If it is possible, as much as it is up to you, be at peace with all men” (Romans 12:18), which means peace is sometimes not within our control. If there is physical, verbal, sexual, or financial abuse and control in the home, that is not something to endure with “goodness.” Safety comes first, and forgiveness does not mean staying in danger. Seek help from a pastor and from professionals right away, and contact local protection services when needed.
83	The last time my spouse’s words stung, what was my first reaction?
84	In which recurring conflict could I be the first to put down the mud?
85	What topic most easily pulls us into tit-for-tat? Could we agree on a signal to pause next time?
86	Looking back on this week, what fruit of goodness have you seen in me? If your spouse can’t join you right now, write down one thing you’d like to thank them for.
87	Next time you want to snap back, take a deep breath and say something gentle
88	Agree with your spouse on a pause signal for times of conflict
89	Pray about a time you were wronged, and entrust it to God
90	“Don’t be overcome by evil, but overcome evil with good” — Lord, right now help me respond with goodness and not be overcome by evil.
