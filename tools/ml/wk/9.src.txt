0	The Source of Love: We Love Because God First Loved Us
1	Go to the Spring
2	Find somewhere near water for a walk: a riverside, a lake, the beach, or a fountain in the park. Talk as you walk about what moved you this week.
3	Each share one specific moment when you knew God loved you. It could be the day you were saved, or something small that happened recently.
4	Take turns finishing this sentence: “Because God loved me first, this week I want to be the first to do this for you: …”
5	Slowly read 1 John 4:19 together, then hold hands and pray: “Lord, be the spring of love in our home.”
6	Broken Cisterns
7	Human love runs dry because we have left the spring of living water
8	Be still and honestly ask yourself: “Lately, how much love is left in my heart?” You don’t need to pretend you’re full. Tell the Lord: “Lord, sometimes I’m dry and tired. Today I want to come back to you, the spring.”
9	321 Light: Treating my spouse as my spring is a hidden form of the self-ruled life: I want you to satisfy me. When they can’t, I become disappointed and blame them. When we turn our focus back to Jesus and drink deeply from him, we stop demanding from our spouse and instead let living water flow out to them.
10	On the greatest day of the feast, Jesus stood and cried out, “If anyone is thirsty, let him come to me and drink!” (John 7:37). He did not scold people for being thirsty; he simply invited them to come to him. Jesus is our example: he himself often withdrew to be with the Father, and he invites the thirsty to return to the source.
11	Spring of living water, we confess that we often leave you and then demand love and approval from each other, and we both end up thirstier. Today we come back to you. Let your love become a spring within us, so that we stop demanding from each other and instead receive from you and let it flow to each other. In the name of the Lord Jesus, amen.
12	Picture a household water tank that has to supply the whole family every day for showers, cooking, and laundry. If the tank isn’t connected to the mains and has only the water first poured into it, then however carefully you use it, one day it will run dry. Many marriages grow weary not because one person is especially bad, but because both are supplying each other from their own limited tank, and it keeps getting lower.
13	Through Jeremiah, God said his people had committed two evils: “they have forsaken me, the spring of living waters, and cut out cisterns for themselves: broken cisterns that can’t hold water” (Jeremiah 2:13). In ancient Israel, living water was flowing spring water, while a cistern was a pit cut into the rock to collect rainwater. People set aside a spring that never stops flowing to rely on a pit they had dug themselves, and a leaky one at that.
14	In marriage we often dig cisterns too: we hope our spouse’s praise, thoughtfulness, and romance will fill our hearts. But our spouse is also a person who needs water; they are not the spring. Jesus told the Samaritan woman, “Everyone who drinks of this water will thirst again” (John 4:13), and also, “the water that I will give him will become in him a well of water springing up to eternal life” (John 4:14). The source of love is not in our spouse; it is in Jesus.
15	Lately, in what area do I most often expect my spouse to “fill” me?
16	When was the last time I was truly filled with love by God? What was that like?
17	How do you think our “water level” has been lately: full, half full, or nearly empty? Each share why.
18	What simple habit could help us return together to God, the spring, every day?
19	Morning and evening today, spend three minutes quietly telling the Lord: “I’m thirsty.”
20	Write down one need you often demand from your spouse, and hand it to God
21	Don’t ask to be loved today; be the first to thank your spouse
22	“whoever drinks of the water that I will give him will never thirst again” — Lord Jesus, right now I come to you to drink. Satisfy my heart.
23	God Is Love
24	Love is defined not by feelings but by the cross
25	Quietly read these three words: “God is love.” Let them settle in your heart. Tell the Lord: “Lord, teach me to stop defining love by my own feelings, and to learn what love is from you.”
26	321 Light: The human view says “love is my feeling,” and the focus is on myself; God’s view says “love is God’s nature,” and the focus is on God. Change your mindset and your life will change: when we learn love from the cross, we stop asking, “Do I still feel it?” and start asking, “How does God want me to love?”
27	In the parable of the prodigal son, Jesus describes how, “while he was still far off, his father saw him and was moved with compassion, and ran, fell on his neck, and kissed him” (Luke 15:20). The father did not wait for his son to prove himself first; he ran to him first. Through this picture Jesus tells us that God’s love always moves first.
28	Heavenly Father, thank you for sending your Son as the atoning sacrifice for our sins, showing us what true love is. Renew our definition of love so that we are no longer led around by our feelings. When we are tired and the feelings fade, remind us of the love of the cross, and let our home be built on your love. Amen.
29	If you want to teach a child what “red” is, the best way isn’t to write an essay; it’s to point to an apple and say, “Look, that’s red.” The Bible teaches us about love the same way. John does not start with an abstract definition; he points to God and says, “God is love” (1 John 4:8). To know what love is, we look at who God is and what God has done.
30	John goes on: “In this is love, not that we loved God, but that he loved us, and sent his Son as the atoning sacrifice for our sins” (1 John 4:10). An “atoning sacrifice” is an offering for sin that turns away God’s righteous anger. In other words, God’s love does not ignore sin; he pays the price himself to deal with it. Love is not an emotional high; it is an action on the cross.
31	This changes the way we see marriage. The world says: love while the feeling lasts, and split up when it’s gone. The Bible says: “love is of God” (1 John 4:7). Our love for our spouse is not manufactured by romance; it is received from God and then lived out. When the tide of feeling goes out, the love of the cross still stands, and that is the true foundation of marriage.
32	Is my inner definition of love closer to a feeling, or to the action of the cross?
33	When I think of “God is love,” what past hurt or experience makes it hard for me to believe it?
34	In our marriage, when have you felt my love not because of romance, but because I paid a price?
35	If you had to describe love with one everyday action, what would you choose? Why?
36	Copy out 1 John 4:10 and put it on your phone’s home screen or the fridge
37	Do one small thing today that costs you something but helps your spouse feel loved
38	Before bed, say together: “God is love, and our home belongs to him.”
39	“God is love” — Father, right now I rest in your love, and I want to carry it to my family.
40	Filled with Love First
41	An empty cup can’t pour; we must be filled with God’s love before we can love our spouse
42	Rest your hands on your knees, palms up, as if holding an empty cup. Quietly tell the Lord: “Lord, I want to love my family, but I find I’m often empty. Today, by your Spirit, pour your love into my heart.”
43	321 Light: When we love our spouse from the head by ourselves, we soon become anxious and worn out; when we live from the heart by the Spirit, love has a source. Life first, then relationship: first let our own lives be filled with God’s love, and our marriage will have strength to be renewed. Change begins with me, and it begins with me returning to God.
44	Before Jesus began his public ministry, at his baptism he heard the Father say, “You are my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased” (Mark 1:11). He had not yet performed a single miracle, yet he was first affirmed by the Father and filled with the Father’s love. Jesus shows us that serving and loving others flow out of our identity as the beloved.
45	Lord, we often try to love in our own strength, and the more we love, the more tired we become. By your Spirit, strengthen us with power in our inner being, so that we may grasp how wide and long and high and deep the love of Christ is. Holy Spirit, pour God’s love abundantly into our hearts, and let our home overflow with your love. Amen.
46	On a plane, the flight attendant always reminds us: “If the oxygen masks drop, put on your own mask before helping the person next to you.” That isn’t selfish; it’s simply true. Without oxygen yourself, you can’t save anyone else. Marriage is the same. We often try to go straight to loving our spouse and forget that we first need to be filled with God’s love.
47	Paul prayed that God would grant the Ephesian church “that you may be strengthened with power through his Spirit in the inner person” (Ephesians 3:16), so that they “may be strengthened to comprehend with all the saints what is the width and length and height and depth” (Ephesians 3:18) of Christ’s love. This “comprehending” is not just head knowledge; it is lived experience. When we truly experience how wide, long, high, and deep Christ’s love is, our hearts are stretched open.
48	Romans says, “God’s love has been poured into our hearts through the Holy Spirit who was given to us” (Romans 5:5). “Poured” means emptied out in abundance, not handed out drop by drop. God does not ration his love stingily; he pours it out lavishly through the Holy Spirit. What we need to do each day is hold our cup before him: reading Scripture, praying, worshiping, being still. Let him fill us, and let it overflow to our family.
49	Lately, have I been forcing myself to love my family while running on an “empty cup”? What are the signs?
50	Do I have a time each day when I truly set my heart before God and let him fill it?
51	When do you feel especially “empty”? How can I come alongside you as you return to God?
52	Could we work out together which time of day each of us, or both of us together, can draw near to God?
53	Tomorrow morning, read a passage of Scripture before you pick up your phone
54	Take one minute today to tell God three ways you have felt his love
55	Ask your spouse: “What can I pray for you today?”
56	“God’s love has been poured into our hearts through the Holy Spirit who was given to us” — Holy Spirit, right now pour God’s love into my heart again.
57	From Demanding Love to Loving First
58	Those forgiven much love much; only those who know they are loved can love first
59	Recall the moment you first understood that your sins were forgiven. Quietly tell the Lord: “Lord, you have forgiven me so much. Today I don’t want to just wait for others to be good to me first.”
60	321 Light: “You first, then me” is the negotiation of the self-ruled life; “God loved me first, so I’ll love you first” is the self-emptied life. Pride provokes pride and leaves us in a standoff; humility inspires humility, and the one who takes the first step often opens the door for both.
61	In everyone’s eyes Zacchaeus was a sinner, yet before he had repented, Jesus looked up and said, “Zacchaeus, hurry and come down, for today I must stay at your house” (Luke 19:5). Jesus accepted him first and entered his house first, and only then was Zacchaeus’ life turned around. Jesus is our example of loving first.
62	Lord, thank you for loving us first, while we were still sinners. We confess that we often wait for the other to change first, and we end up at a standstill. Help us know more deeply how greatly we have been forgiven, so that our love may grow too, and make us willing to take the first step: to be gentle first, to apologize first, to embrace first. Amen.
63	Imagine two people at a door, each waiting for the other to open it. One says, “Apologize first, and I’ll forgive you.” The other says, “Be gentle first, and I won’t be harsh.” Both have a point, yet both are still standing outside. Many couples get stuck exactly like this, not because they don’t want love, but because each is waiting for the other to give first.
64	Simon the Pharisee invited Jesus to dinner but gave him no water for his feet, while a sinful woman wet Jesus’ feet with her tears. Jesus said, “her sins, which are many, are forgiven, for she loved much. But one to whom little is forgiven, loves little” (Luke 7:47). Her love was not a way to earn forgiveness. She knew she had been forgiven much, and love naturally poured out of her.
65	John puts it simply: “We love him, because he first loved us” (1 John 4:19). God is always the one who moves first. The more I understand how much I owed God and how much grace I have received, the less I will keep score with my spouse about who goes first. Moving from “you have to love me first” to “I’m willing to love you first” is not losing out; it is the natural response of someone who is loved.
66	In what matter have I been waiting for my spouse to change first, apologize first, or give first?
67	Do I feel I have been “forgiven little,” and so my love for my spouse is little too?
68	Is there something where you’d like us to stop waiting on each other and both take the first step together?
69	If I were to do one thing first today to show my love, what would you most like it to be? If your spouse can’t join you for now, write down one thing you want to do for them first.
70	In one small matter today, don’t wait for your spouse; go first
71	Tell your spouse: “Thank you. I want to do this for you first.”
72	Write down three things God has forgiven you for, and thank him
73	“We love him, because he first loved us” — Lord, you loved me first; right now I choose to love first.
74	Remaining in Love
75	When we remain in the Lord’s love, love is no longer forced but flows from life
76	Look back over this week: broken cisterns, God is love, being filled with love, loving first. Be still for a moment and tell the Lord: “Lord, I don’t want to just visit you now and then. I want to live in your love.”
77	321 Light: Fear often makes us cling tightly to control, which is one face of the self-ruled life. Remaining in God’s love means letting Jesus reign; instead of trading control for security, we find our security in his love. The self-emptied are not afraid of losing themselves, so they are free to love.
78	However busy Jesus was, he often returned to the Father: “Early in the morning, while it was still dark, he rose up and went out, and departed into a deserted place, and prayed there” (Mark 1:35). His whole life was lived in the Father’s love, which is why he could keep on loving in the face of crowds, misunderstanding, and the cross.
79	Lord Jesus, thank you for loving us just as the Father has loved you. Help us remain in your love, no longer bound by fear and no longer protecting ourselves through control. Let your perfect love drive out the fear in our hearts, and make our home a place where your love lives. Amen.
80	Picture a grape branch. It never grits its teeth trying to produce fruit; it needs only one thing: to stay attached to the vine. Sap flows into the branch naturally, and fruit grows naturally. Jesus said, “Even as the Father has loved me, I also have loved you. Remain in my love” (John 15:9). To “remain” means to settle in and not leave. Love in marriage is like this too: it isn’t sustained by one big sprint, but by a lasting connection.
81	John says, “he who remains in love remains in God, and God remains in him” (1 John 4:16). Then he adds, “There is no fear in love; but perfect love casts out fear” (1 John 4:18). Behind many marital conflicts is actually fear: fear of being looked down on, fear of being abandoned, fear of not being good enough. As we remain in God’s perfect love, these fears gradually lose their power to control us.
82	This week we have seen that human love runs dry and God alone is the source; that love is defined by the cross; and that we must first be filled before we can love first. Finally, Jesus ties it all together: “love one another, even as I have loved you” (John 15:12). This is not a heavy command; it is what a person who lives in love naturally looks like. Our home can become a place where God’s love settles and lives.
