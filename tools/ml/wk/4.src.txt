0	A Great Mystery: Christ and the Church
1	Seeing Him Through Us
2	Go together to a place with a far view (a hilltop, the beach, a tall building, or a park), or simply stand at a window at home, and spend five quiet minutes enjoying what God has made.
3	Take turns sharing: what in our marriage has shown you the love of Jesus?
4	Imagine yourselves twenty years from now, and each finish this sentence: “By then, I hope people will see in us...”
5	Read Ephesians 5:32 together and pray: “Lord, may our marriage be a picture of your love for the church.”
6	Marriage Is a Picture
7	Earthly marriage is a shadow that points to Christ and the church
8	Be still and think of the photo your family treasures most. Then tell the Lord: “Lord, let our marriage also be a photo that helps people see you.”
9	321 Light: Thinking marriage exists just to meet my needs is seeing it from the human point of view; seeing that marriage exists to display Christ is seeing it from God’s point of view. When our focus turns from ourselves to Jesus, marriage moves from our small “us” into God’s eternal purpose.
10	Jesus said: “The Kingdom of Heaven is like a certain king, who made a wedding feast for his son” (Matthew 22:2). He often used wedding feasts to explain the kingdom, because marriage best expresses the joy, intimacy, and commitment between God and his people. Jesus shows us that every wedding is a preview of a far greater wedding.
11	Lord Jesus, thank you for revealing this great mystery, showing us that marriage speaks of you and the church. Our picture isn’t perfect; please restore it yourself. Help us nourish and cherish each other, so that people catch a glimpse in our home of how you love the church. Amen.
12	However beautiful your vacation photos are, they aren’t the scenery itself; yet a good photo can make someone who’s never been there want to go and see for themselves. Paul says earthly marriage is a photo like that. After quoting Genesis 2:24, he goes on: “This mystery is great, but I speak concerning Christ and the assembly” (Ephesians 5:32), that is, the church. When God established marriage, he already had another marriage in mind.
13	In the Bible, a “mystery” isn’t something strange and hard to understand; it’s something once hidden and now revealed. People used to think marriage was simply a man and a woman uniting and starting a family. Now, through the gospel, we see clearly that God established marriage as a picture anyone can understand, portraying how Christ loves the church. Paul says that a man “nourishes and cherishes” his own body, “even as the Lord also does the assembly” (Ephesians 5:29), “because we are members of his body” (Ephesians 5:30).
14	This changes how we see marriage. Our marriage isn’t just “the two of us”; it’s a picture God has hung up in the world. The picture may be stained or torn, but its subject hasn’t changed: Christ loves the church. So whenever we nourish and cherish each other, we add a brushstroke to the picture, helping people catch a glimpse of the far more beautiful Bridegroom.
15	What did I used to think was the main purpose of marriage? How is that different from what Paul says?
16	If people could only come to know Christ through our marriage, what kind of Christ would they see?
17	In the picture that is our marriage, which brushstroke do you think best shows people the love of Jesus?
18	In what way do I “nourish and cherish” you that you feel most? Where would you like a little more?
19	Today, “nourish and cherish” your spouse with one concrete action, like bringing a glass of water or rubbing their shoulders
20	Memorize Ephesians 5:32
21	Choose a photo of the two of you and write on the back: “May people see Christ in us.”
22	“This mystery is great, but I speak concerning Christ and the assembly” — Lord, right now may our marriage be a picture that points to you.
23	God, the Bridegroom Who Pursues His Bride
24	The whole Bible tells of a Bridegroom pursuing the bride he loves
25	Close your eyes and hear God say to you: “I will betroth you to me forever” (Hosea 2:19). However unlovable you feel today, let these words reach you first; then, with a heart that knows it is loved, go and love your spouse.
26	321 Light: Behind many demands in marriage lies the emptiness of “I’m not loved enough.” The self-ruled heart tries to fill that emptiness from its spouse, and both end up exhausted. When we are first filled with God’s love, we stop taking from our spouse and can give out of the overflow.
27	When people asked why Jesus’ disciples didn’t fast, Jesus answered: “Can the groomsmen fast while the bridegroom is with them?” (Mark 2:19). Jesus called himself the bridegroom. He came to bring his bride home, and his presence brings the joy of a wedding feast.
28	Lord, you are the Bridegroom who pursues us, winning us back with righteousness, justice, loving kindness, compassion, and faithfulness. Thank you for rejoicing over us as a bridegroom rejoices over his bride. Fill us with your love, so that we stop taking from each other and love each other out of your abundance. Amen.
29	Some have said the Bible is a great love story: it begins with a wedding in Eden and ends with the wedding feast of the Lamb. In the Old Testament, God often describes his relationship with his people as that of a husband and a bridegroom. Even when Israel left him again and again, God still said through the prophet Hosea: “I will betroth you to me forever” (Hosea 2:19).
30	In ancient times, the bridegroom paid a bride-price at the betrothal. What bride-price did God pay to win his people? “I will betroth you to me in righteousness, in justice, in loving kindness, and in compassion” (Hosea 2:19), and “I will even betroth you to me in faithfulness” (Hosea 2:20). These are all God’s own attributes. God doesn’t wait for his bride to become worthy of him first; he gives everything he has to win back those who are unworthy.
31	Isaiah says: “As a bridegroom rejoices over his bride, so your God will rejoice over you” (Isaiah 62:5). Picture the look in a groom’s eyes as his bride walks in at the wedding; that is the delight with which God looks at us. We can love our spouse well because we have first been loved like this. Only those who are loved have the strength to love.
32	Do I truly believe that God looks at me with the delight of a bridegroom looking at his bride? What makes that hard for me to believe?
33	Which of my recent demands on my spouse are really attempts to get from him/her a love only God can give?
34	Do you remember something I did while we were dating that made you feel “pursued”?
35	In our life now, how could I help you feel cherished and pursued again?
36	Today, give your spouse a small surprise, just like when you were dating
37	Say to yourself three times in your heart: “God delights in me.”
38	Write your spouse a card naming one thing you treasure about him/her
39	“As a bridegroom rejoices over his bride, so your God will rejoice over you” — Lord, right now, resting in your delight, let me delight in my spouse too.
40	Not Just Happiness, but Holiness
41	God uses marriage to shape us into the likeness of Christ
42	Think of the thing in your marriage that has made you most uncomfortable lately. Don’t rush to work out who’s right and who’s wrong; simply ask the Lord: “Lord, what do you want to do in me through this?”
43	321 Light: The self-ruled heart only wants to change its spouse so that it can be comfortable; the self-emptied heart lets God change it through its spouse. Friction in marriage isn’t necessarily a sign that the marriage is failing. It may be God wearing away our “self” so that the likeness of Christ can show through.
44	Jesus prayed for his disciples: “For their sakes I sanctify myself, that they themselves also may be sanctified in truth” (John 17:19). Jesus first gave himself wholly to the Father so that his disciples could be made holy too. In marriage, we don’t start by demanding that our spouse become holy; we start by offering ourselves, becoming a help to our spouse’s growth in holiness.
45	Holy God, thank you for wanting not only our happiness but for us to become like your Son. We admit that we often want only to change each other, and are unwilling to be changed by you. Use every adjustment in our marriage to wear away our pride and self-will, so that the likeness of Christ grows clearer and clearer in us. Amen.
46	A rough stone in a river is washed by the current day after day, knocking against other stones, until at last it becomes a smooth, round pebble. Marriage is like that river. Many people marry to find happiness. There’s nothing wrong with that; God is glad for us to have joy. But God established marriage for a deeper purpose as well: to make us holy.
47	Paul says: “all things work together for good for those who love God” (Romans 8:28). What is this “good”? The next verse explains: “For whom he foreknew, he also predestined to be conformed to the image of his Son” (Romans 8:29). The greatest good isn’t everything going smoothly; it’s becoming more and more like Christ. The adjustments, the waiting, the misunderstandings, and the reconciliations of marriage can all become tools God uses to shape us.
48	Paul also describes Christ giving himself up for the church “that he might sanctify her” (Ephesians 5:26), so that in the end she would be “holy and without defect” (Ephesians 5:27). This is Christ’s heart for the church, and it is the direction of marriage too. When we ask, “Is this marriage making me happy?” we can also ask, “Is this marriage making me more like Jesus?”
49	Which recent friction in our marriage might be God’s opportunity to wear down one of my rough edges?
50	Do I often treat “happiness” as the only measure of our marriage?
51	Since we married, in what way do you think I’ve become more like Jesus? (Please name one specific thing.)
52	What kind of friction comes up between us most often? How can we hand it over to God together for him to use?
53	Write down one rough edge in your character that you’re willing to let God change through your marriage
54	When friction comes up today, first ask in your heart: “Lord, what do you want to change in me?”
55	Tell your spouse one virtue you’ve learned from him/her
56	“all things work together for good for those who love God” — Lord, right now I trust that you are using our marriage to make me more like Jesus.
57	Letting People Read Christ in Our Home
58	Our marriage is a letter from Christ, read by everyone
59	Imagine a letter lying open on your dining table, and written in it is how the two of you lived this week. Quietly ask the Lord: “Lord, if people read this letter, would they come to know you?”
60	321 Light: The purpose of marriage isn’t just to build a “good family,” but to build a household that belongs to God and let Jesus receive all the glory. The self-ruled home wants people to see how good it is; the self-emptied home lets people see how good Jesus is. Change from the inside out will in time bring change to our relationships and our ministry.
61	Jesus went to stay at Zacchaeus’s house, and that day his whole household was changed. Jesus said: “Today, salvation has come to this house” (Luke 19:9). No home Jesus enters stays the same. When we invite Jesus to reign in our home, our home too will become a place where others see salvation.
62	Holy Spirit, giver of life, write on the pages of our hearts yourself. May our home be a letter from Christ, letting people see not our goodness but the goodness of Jesus. In our imperfection, show your grace; in our everyday life, let people come to know you. Amen.
63	In Paul’s day, a preacher arriving in a new place often carried a letter of recommendation to prove he could be trusted. Some questioned Paul because he had no such letter, but Paul said: “You are our letter, written in our hearts, known and read by all men” (2 Corinthians 3:2). The changed lives of believers were the best letter of recommendation.
64	Paul goes on: “you are a letter of Christ” (2 Corinthians 3:3), a letter “written not with ink, but with the Spirit of the living God” (2 Corinthians 3:3). Our home is a letter too. Our children are reading it, our neighbors are reading it, our coworkers, relatives, and friends are reading it. They may not read the Bible, but they read us every day: how we make up after a fight, how we hold each other up when we’re weak.
65	This doesn’t mean we have to pretend to be perfect. In a real letter, what moves people most is often how God works in the midst of imperfection: a home that once lived in cold silence learning to apologize, a couple who were once worn out praying together again. Let the Holy Spirit write this letter; all we need to do is hand him the pages of our hearts every day. Then what people read in us isn’t how good we are, but how good Christ is.
66	If our family’s life this week were written up as a letter, what words would appear most often?
67	In front of my family, children, relatives, and friends, what about Christ do I most hope they will read in me?
68	What do you think our relatives, children, or neighbors are most likely to read in our marriage?
69	Is there someone we’d like to bless together with our lives, so that they can see Christ?
70	Today, in front of your family or children, say a word of thanks or affirmation to your spouse
71	If there’s been conflict lately, take the first step to make peace today, so your family can see what reconciliation looks like
72	Pray for a relative or friend you’d like to bless
73	“you are a letter of Christ” — Lord, right now write your love in my life.
74	From the Temporary to the Eternal
75	Earthly marriage will end, but the eternity it points to never will
76	Look back over this month: marriage was established by God, your spouse is God’s gift, your covenant was made before God, and marriage points to Christ. Now quietly lift your eyes and tell the Lord: “Lord, let me see today’s marriage with eternity in view.”
77	321 Light: From earth to heaven, from the temporary to the eternal, from the worldly to the godly. When our eyes are on the temporary, we easily keep score and lose heart; when our eyes are on the eternal, we can endure and hope. Our home is a home on earth getting ready to welcome the Bridegroom.
78	Jesus told his disciples: “I am going to prepare a place for you” (John 14:2). In the wedding customs of the time, a newly betrothed bridegroom would go back to prepare a home, then return for his bride when the time came. Jesus is that faithful Bridegroom. He promised, “I will come again and will receive you to myself” (John 14:3). This is the ultimate hope of our marriage.
79	Eternal Lord, thank you for showing us that earthly marriage is a shadow pointing to you. Help us look not only at what is seen right in front of us, but at what is eternal; and in today’s small details and difficulties, keep us walking toward you together. May our home be a home waiting for the Bridegroom, until you come again. Amen.
80	No one decorates an airport departure lounge as if it were home, because they know they’re about to leave. But no one trashes the lounge or picks fights with the people around them either, because they’re traveling together. Jesus said: “in the resurrection they neither marry nor are given in marriage” (Matthew 22:30). Earthly marriage has a beginning and an end; it is a companion for the journey, not the final destination.
81	This doesn’t diminish marriage; it makes marriage even more precious. If marriage is a picture pointing to Christ and the church, then when the real wedding feast comes, the picture will have fulfilled its purpose. Paul says: “the things which are seen are temporal, but the things which are not seen are eternal” (2 Corinthians 4:18). The pain in marriage is real, but seen from eternity, it is “our light affliction, which is for the moment” (2 Corinthians 4:17).
82	Looking at marriage from a temporary point of view, we ask: “Am I happy right now?” Looking from an eternal point of view, we ask: “Are we moving toward Christ together?” This month we’ve traveled from Genesis to Ephesians and seen that God is the designer of marriage. God designed it, joined it, witnesses it, and in the end will use it to point to the wedding feast of the Lamb. Let’s carry this eternal perspective into every week ahead.
