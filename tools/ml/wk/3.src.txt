0	A Covenant, Not a Contract: What God Has Joined
1	Signs of Our Covenant
2	Look together at your wedding rings (or your marriage certificate or wedding photos), and each share: when you said “I do,” what was going through your mind?
3	Take turns sharing one time when “we might have given up, but we didn’t,” and tell how God kept you through it.
4	Write together a covenant statement of your own, for example: “In good times and bad, we choose each other, and we choose Jesus.”
5	Hold hands and read Matthew 19:6, then pray: “Lord, you joined us together. Be the witness and the keeper of our covenant.”
6	Contract and Covenant
7	A contract is about conditions; a covenant is about commitment
8	Be still and consider: in our marriage, am I hiding some “I’ll do it only if you...” in my heart? You don’t need to rush to change it; just lay it honestly before the Lord.
9	321 Light: The contract mindset is centered on me, always calculating gains and losses and protecting myself; that is exactly the self-ruled life. The covenant mindset puts the focus on God and on my spouse, asking first “How can I keep the covenant?” rather than “Have you kept it?” Change your mindset and your life will change.
10	At the Last Supper, Jesus took the cup and said: “This cup is the new covenant in my blood, which is poured out for you” (Luke 22:20). His disciples were about to run away and deny him, yet Jesus still made a covenant with his own life. Jesus’ love is not a contract; it is a covenant guaranteed with his blood.
11	Covenant-keeping God of steadfast love, thank you for walking alone between those pieces, and for making a new covenant with us through the blood of Jesus. We admit that we often calculate gains and losses with a contract heart. Renew us, so that our marriage is built not on conditions but on your faithfulness, and we stay committed to each other for life. Amen.
12	When you rent a home you sign a contract: the landlord provides the house, the tenant pays rent on time, and if either side breaks the terms, the contract can be ended. At the heart of a contract is the word “if”: if you do your part, I’ll do mine. Without realizing it, many people run their marriage with a contract mindset: “I’ll be good to you if you’re good to me; if you won’t change, I don’t have to either.” But the marriage God established is not a contract. It is a covenant.
13	Genesis 15 records an ancient covenant ceremony. The animals were cut in two, and Abram “laid each half opposite the other” (Genesis 15:10). By the custom of the day, both parties would walk between the pieces, as if to say: “If I break this covenant, may I become like these animals.” It was a commitment guaranteed with one’s life. In fact, the Hebrew expression for making a covenant literally means “to cut a covenant.”
14	The amazing thing is that on that day only “a smoking furnace and a flaming torch passed between these pieces” (Genesis 15:17), the sign of God’s presence, while Abram lay in a deep sleep. God walked through alone, as if to say: “The cost of keeping this covenant, I will bear by myself.” At the heart of a covenant is not “if” but “I will”: whatever you are like, I commit myself to you. This is how God treats us, and it is the foundation of marriage.
15	Which of the things I do for my spouse actually come with the condition “You be good to me first, then I’ll be good to you”?
16	In what situations do I most often “keep score” in my heart of who gives more in our marriage?
17	Where in our marriage do you think things look more like a “contract” and need to turn back into a “covenant” together?
18	Was there a time when you felt me standing by you unconditionally? How did that feel?
19	Today, do one thing to love your spouse without expecting anything in return
20	When you notice yourself thinking “I’ll do it only if you...,” change it to “I will...”
21	Write “A covenant, not a contract” in the notes app on your phone
22	“This cup is the new covenant in my blood, which is poured out for you” — Lord Jesus, thank you for making a covenant with me with your life. Teach me to love like this too.
23	God Stands Between Us as Witness
24	The marriage covenant is made before God; God honors the covenant and protects the oppressed
25	Imagine God sitting right between the two of you, looking at you with both tenderness and justice. Quietly tell him: “Lord, you are the witness of our marriage covenant. I want to be honest before you.”
26	321 Light: Dealing treacherously is what the self-ruled heart does for its own convenience, sacrificing the other person in the covenant. To let God be God is to acknowledge that my marriage has a witness; to let people be people is to treat my spouse with honesty, respect, and protection, never oppressing and never hiding.
27	Jesus said: “let your ‘Yes’ be ‘Yes’ and your ‘No’ be ‘No’” (Matthew 5:37). His whole life, his words matched his actions; he never covered one way of living with nice-sounding words. Jesus shows us that for someone who lives before God, a promise is a promise, with no need for extra oaths to prove it.
28	God of justice and love, thank you for being the witness of our marriage covenant and for watching over everyone who is wronged and oppressed. Guard our hearts so that we never treat each other with treachery or violence; make our home the safest place for each other. And protect every family that is in danger right now; open a way out for them. Amen.
29	In Malachi’s day, the people wept at the altar and couldn’t understand why God no longer accepted their offerings. The prophet answered: “Because the LORD has been witness between you and the wife of your youth” (Malachi 2:14). In those days many husbands casually divorced the wives they had married when young. God was saying: how you treat your spouse directly affects your relationship with me.
30	God calls her “your companion and the wife of your covenant” (Malachi 2:14). The marriage covenant isn’t a private agreement between two people; it is a covenant made before God, and he is the witness. Just as a notary is present when a contract is signed, God himself witnesses a marriage. So the prophet warns twice: “take heed to your spirit” (Malachi 2:15). Keeping covenant doesn’t begin with big things; it begins with guarding the small thoughts of the heart.
31	Verse 16 is translated in different ways, but its message is clear. The WEB puts it this way: “One who hates and divorces... covers his garment with violence!” (Malachi 2:16). God hates covenant-breaking, and he hates violence just as much. So let it be said plainly: physical, verbal, sexual, and financial abuse and control are in themselves a breaking of the covenant, and God does not want anyone to stay in danger and endure it. If you are in a situation like this, your safety comes first. Please seek help from a pastor and from professionals as soon as possible.
32	If God were sitting right between us, which of my recent words or attitudes would make me feel ashamed?
33	Are there small thoughts in my heart slowly wearing down my commitment to our marriage covenant?
34	When you think of “God standing between us as witness,” what comforts you most?
35	In our home, what kinds of words or actions make you feel most safe? If your spouse can’t join you for now, write down the kind of safety you hope to create for him or her.
36	Pay attention to your tone of voice today, and use no mocking or threatening words
37	Tell your spouse: “I remember what I said before God.”
38	If someone you know is in a dangerous marriage, pray for them and go with them to seek help
39	“Therefore take heed to your spirit” — Lord, right now guard my heart, so that I keep covenant honestly before you.
40	The Weight of a Vow
41	“I do” is a promise we spend a lifetime keeping
42	Recall the “I do” you spoke at your wedding. However moved or nervous you were when you said it, gently bring it before the Lord: “Lord, today I want to hear again how much these words weigh.”
43	321 Light: The self-ruled heart keeps its vows by asking whether it’s worth it for me; the self-emptied heart keeps its vows by asking whether God receives the glory. Our vows aren’t our achievement; they are a response of faith. We say “I do” because we trust the God who makes us able.
44	In the garden of Gethsemane, Jesus prayed: “not my will, but yours, be done” (Luke 22:42). He knew the cross lay ahead and the cost was enormous, yet he did not take back his commitment. Jesus shows us that a true “I will” is saying “I will” even at the hardest moment.
45	Lord, we once said “I do” before you. Thank you for always remembering those words and for always helping us keep them. In good times, keep us from taking them lightly; in hard times, keep us from giving up. By your grace, may every promise we have spoken become true. Amen.
46	Wedding vows are usually only a few lines: “For better, for worse, for richer, for poorer, in sickness and in health, to love and to cherish, till death do us part.” Saying them takes less than a minute, but keeping them takes a lifetime. It’s like signing a blank check: you don’t know what amount will be filled in later, but the moment you sign, you’ve already agreed.
47	The Teacher says: “When you vow a vow to God, don’t defer to pay it” (Ecclesiastes 5:4), and again: “It is better that you should not vow, than that you should vow and not pay” (Ecclesiastes 5:5). In the Bible a vow is serious business, because it is spoken to God. Wedding vows aren’t spoken only to our spouse; they are spoken before God. When we’re tempted to cut corners on them, we should remember that it wasn’t only our spouse who heard those words. God heard them too.
48	The psalm describes the one who may live on God’s holy hill: “he who keeps an oath even when it hurts, and doesn’t change” (Psalm 15:4). The worth of a vow shows most clearly when keeping it hurts. Keeping covenant in good times isn’t hard; keeping it in hard times reveals how much a vow weighs. Of course, keeping covenant isn’t a matter of gritting our teeth and toughing it out. It means returning to God every day and asking for his strength, so that the words we spoke become true one day at a time.

55	Today, follow through on one small thing you promised your spouse
56	“he who keeps an oath even when it hurts, and doesn’t change” — Lord, right now make the “I do” I once spoke still count today.
57	Not Giving Up Easily
58	We can hold on because God never gives up on us
59	If you feel weary right now, even a little like giving up, don’t blame yourself. Just hand this weariness to the Lord and tell him: “Lord, I’m tired, but I know you have never given up on me.”
60	321 Light: The urge to give up often comes from a misplaced focus: looking only at my spouse’s failures, my own grievances, and my present circumstances. When our focus is on Jesus, we look past the temporary to the eternal. God is still at work and the story isn’t finished yet, so we have reason to hold on one more day and love one more time.
61	As Jesus was about to leave his disciples, he promised: “I will not leave you orphans. I will come to you” (John 14:18). His disciples were about to fail and scatter, yet Jesus did not take back his promise. He never gives up on the weak, and that gives us courage to keep holding on in our marriage.
62	Faithful God, thank you for never withdrawing your loving kindness and never breaking your covenant. When we are weary, disappointed, and ready to give up, hold us up yourself, so that we choose each other once again. And protect all who are in danger; bring them safety and help. Amen.
63	When a child learns to ride a bike, the parent holds the bike from behind. If the child falls, the parent doesn’t say, “You’re hopeless, give up.” They pick the child up and try again. God treats his people the same way. In Psalm 89, God says that even if David’s descendants forsake his law, he will discipline them, “But I will not completely take my loving kindness from him, nor allow my faithfulness to fail” (Psalm 89:33).
64	God goes on: “I will not break my covenant, nor alter what my lips have uttered” (Psalm 89:34). Paul also says: “If we are faithless, he remains faithful; for he can’t deny himself” (2 Timothy 2:13). We can hold on in marriage not because we’re especially strong, but because the God we trust is a God who doesn’t give up. The way he treats us becomes our model for how we treat our spouse.
65	Not giving up easily means that in seasons of disappointment, conflict, and coldness, we don’t make “leaving” our first option, but go back to God together and seek restoration. But this does not mean staying in danger. If there is violence, threats, or control in a marriage, protecting your own safety and your children’s is the responsible choice. Forgiveness and reconciliation also have to happen on the basis of safety, step by step, with pastors and professionals walking alongside.

74	A Covenant Written on the Heart
75	The power to keep covenant comes not from willpower but from the new heart God gives
76	This week we’ve seen the weight of covenant, and perhaps we’ve also seen our own weakness. Be still, put your hand on your heart, and tell the Lord: “Lord, I can’t keep this on my own. Please write your covenant on my heart.”
77	321 Light: Keeping covenant in my own strength is another form of self on the throne: thinking that if I just try hard enough, I can do it. Being self-emptied means admitting I can’t, and letting Christ live in me. It isn’t that I kept the covenant; it’s that the covenant-keeping God kept us.
78	Scripture says: “having loved his own who were in the world, he loved them to the end” (John 13:1). Jesus knew Judas would betray him and Peter would deny him, yet he loved them to the end. His love isn’t an occasional feeling; it’s a commitment that lasts to the end. This is the love he wants to place in our hearts through the Holy Spirit.
79	God of the new covenant, thank you for not giving up on us when we broke covenant, but instead writing your covenant on our hearts through the blood of Jesus. We admit that we can’t keep this covenant on our own. Holy Spirit, renew us every day, so that love grows from within us and we walk with you and with each other all our lives. Amen.
80	Many people make New Year’s resolutions to exercise or go to bed early, then give up within a few weeks. Willpower alone rarely lasts. The marriage covenant is the same. This week we’ve learned about covenant, vows, and not giving up, but if we just listen and then grit our teeth to do it, we’ll soon wear out. What we need isn’t just more determination, but a new heart.
81	Through Jeremiah, God said that Israel had broken the covenant: “which covenant of mine they broke, although I was a husband to them” (Jeremiah 31:32). God didn’t give up there; instead he promised a new covenant: “I will put my law in their inward parts, and I will write it in their heart” (Jeremiah 31:33). The old covenant was carved on tablets of stone, making demands from the outside; the new covenant is written on the heart, changing us from the inside.
82	This is the gospel: Jesus’ death dealt with the sin of our covenant-breaking, Jesus’ resurrection gives us new life, and the Holy Spirit lives in us, making us willing and able to keep covenant. Jesus said: “What therefore God has joined together, don’t let man tear apart” (Matthew 19:6). It is God who joined us together, and it is God who gives us strength from within to live out this covenant day by day.
