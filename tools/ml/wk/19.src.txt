0	Fruit of the Spirit (7): Faithfulness
1	Retracing the Footprints of Faithfulness
2	Get paper and pens. Each of you write down three moments in your marriage when “God did not abandon us,” then take turns sharing.
3	Each of you name one thing the other “said and then did” that gave you a deep sense of security, and thank them specifically.
4	Together, set a small commitment you can keep next week (for example, a walk together on Wednesday). Write it on the calendar and check on it next week.
5	Read Lamentations 3:22–23 together and declare as one: “They are new every morning. Great is your faithfulness” (Lamentations 3:23).
6	New Every Morning
7	God’s faithfulness is the foundation of faithfulness in marriage
8	You woke this morning, breathed, opened your eyes, and everything around you was a new day again. Quietly tell the Lord: “Lord, thank you for coming again this morning. Your faithfulness has never once been late.”
9	321 Light: Human faithfulness rises and falls with our emotions, because the self-ruled life is loyal only to “my feelings.” When our focus shifts from self to God, from the temporary to the eternal, we stop asking only, “Do I still feel it?” and start asking, “How does the faithful God want me to keep my covenant today?”
10	John says of Jesus, “having loved his own who were in the world, he loved them to the end” (John 13:1). That night, Judas would betray him, Peter would deny him, and the other disciples would run away. Jesus knew it all, yet he loved them to the end. Jesus’ faithfulness didn’t depend on his disciples being reliable; it flowed from who he is—faithful.
11	Faithful God, thank you that your loving kindness is new every morning. In our days of failure, weariness, and cooling feelings, you have never left. Let our marriage be built not on changing feelings but on your unchanging faithfulness. Let us receive it from you, and live it out toward each other. Amen.
12	Lamentations is a book written among ruins. Jerusalem had been destroyed, its gates burned, its people taken captive. Yet right in the middle of the darkest chapter, the prophet says, “This I recall to my mind; therefore I have hope” (Lamentations 3:21). What he recalled was not his own strength but God’s character. When a marriage hits hard times, this is what we most need to recall too.
13	He says that God’s loving kindnesses and mercies “are new every morning. Great is your faithfulness” (Lamentations 3:23). “Faithfulness” here means steady and dependable, like a rock that cannot be crushed. The sun never goes on strike and refuses to rise today just because clouds covered it yesterday. God’s love for us doesn’t depend on how well we did yesterday; it arrives fresh every morning.
14	This is the foundation of faithfulness in marriage. If a couple’s loyalty rests only on their feelings—faithful when the feelings are strong, wavering when they fade—the marriage is built on sand. But when we first receive God’s faithfulness each day, we have strength to be faithful to our spouse. We can keep our covenant not because we are so reliable, but because the God who made a covenant with us never breaks his word.
15	Is my loyalty in marriage built more on my feelings or on God’s faithfulness?
16	Thinking back on a hard season in our marriage, where did God’s faithfulness hold me up?
17	In our marriage, what is your most unforgettable experience of “God did not abandon us”?
18	Was there a day recently when you especially needed the promise that his mercies are “new every morning”?
19	When you get up in the morning, first tell God: “Thank you for your faithfulness.”
20	Write down one way God has faithfully held up your marriage
21	Tell your spouse: “Thank you for staying by my side all these years.”
22	“They are new every morning. Great is your faithfulness” — Faithful Lord, right now I give today to you; you never break your word.
23	Let Your Yes Be Yes
24	Faithfulness means doing what we say, even when it costs us
25	Think back over the things you have already promised people today, big and small. Quietly ask the Lord: “Lord, make my ‘yes’ a real ‘yes,’ so that my family can rely on my word with confidence.”
26	321 Light: Behind frequently broken promises there is often the self-ruled life: I keep my word only when it’s convenient, and when it isn’t, my own needs jump to the front of the line. Self-emptied faithfulness puts “I promised you” ahead of “what I want right now.” People matter more than tasks; relationships matter more than convenience.
27	Jesus “intently set his face to go to Jerusalem” (Luke 9:51), because he knew it was the mission the Father had entrusted to him, even with the cross ahead. In the end he could say, “I have accomplished the work which you have given me to do” (John 17:4). Jesus did what he said, and no cost could make him change.
28	Lord Jesus, you accomplished everything the Father entrusted to you, and every word you speak can be trusted. Forgive us for the many times we have promised lightly and not followed through. Help us speak honestly, promise carefully, and do our best to finish what we’ve agreed to. Let our “yes” be a “yes” the other can lean on with confidence. Amen.
29	Whether a check is worth anything depends not on the number written on it but on whether there’s money in the bank. The words we say to our spouse are checks too: “I’ll be home by seven,” “I’ll spend this weekend with you,” “I’ll change.” If they keep bouncing, however nice our words sound, our spouse no longer dares to cash them.
30	Jesus said, “But let your ‘Yes’ be ‘Yes’ and your ‘No’ be ‘No’” (Matthew 5:37). In those days people used all sorts of oaths to add weight to their words, as if anything said without an oath could be discounted. But Jesus said every word of God’s people should be reliable, needing no extra guarantee. At home, that means doing what we’ve agreed to do, saying honestly when we can’t, and not brushing our spouse off with “Yeah, yeah, sure.”
31	The psalm describes the person God welcomes as one “who keeps an oath even when it hurts, and doesn’t change” (Psalm 15:4). Our wedding covenant is an oath we made before God and many witnesses. Over time, keeping it can feel costly: we have to set aside our own plans and give more patience. But it is exactly this kind of faithfulness—keeping our word even when it hurts—that gives our spouse’s heart a place to rest.
32	What is the most recent promise to my spouse that “bounced”? Why didn’t I follow through?
33	Do I often brush my spouse off with “Yeah, yeah, sure”? What attitude lies behind that?
34	What is something I said and then did that made you feel especially secure?
35	Is there a small promise I often make but don’t keep? Please tell me gently—I want to start again.
36	Today, only promise what you’re sure you can do
37	Finish one small thing you promised your spouse but haven’t yet done
38	If you can’t do something, say so honestly and up front, without making excuses
39	“let your ‘Yes’ be ‘Yes’ and your ‘No’ be ‘No.’” — Lord, right now make my words honest and reliable.
40	Faithful in the Smallest Things
41	Great trust is built up from small things
42	Look at something small near you: a cup, a key, a pen. Quietly wonder: does God care about things this small too? Tell the Lord: “Lord, today I want to be faithful to you in small things.”
43	321 Light: In the practice of 920 (my spouse is my most important “2”; practice the “0” of self-emptying; bear the “9”-fold fruit), faithfulness is an “unremarkable” fruit, yet it is the foundation of every relationship. Life first, then relationships, then ministry: faithfulness in small things at home is the very training ground for the greater ministry God will entrust to us later.
44	Even in his extreme agony on the cross, Jesus remembered his mother and entrusted her to the disciple he loved: “Woman, behold, your son!” (John 19:26). While accomplishing the great work of redemption, he did not overlook the needs of those beside him. Jesus was faithful in the greatest matter, and faithful in what seemed small.
45	Lord, you value the smallest things, and you are faithful to us even in the smallest things. Help us be faithful with our time, our money, our words, and our small promises. Shine your light on every place we have hidden or been careless, and make us willing to be open. Let our trust in each other be built up day by day. Amen.
46	How does a piggy bank get filled? Not with one big sum dropped in at once, but coin by coin. Trust in marriage works the same way. Replying to messages promptly, remembering what your spouse asked you to do, keeping the finances clear, not being late when you’ve agreed on a time—these seem trivial, but bit by bit they build the trust that says, “I can safely entrust myself to you.”
47	Jesus said, “He who is faithful in a very little is faithful also in much” (Luke 16:10). We think that only when a big test comes can you tell whether someone is faithful, but Jesus says the real score was set long before, in the small things. Someone who is sloppy in small matters, vague about small sums of money, and careless about small lies will find it very hard to stand firm when a big temptation comes.
48	Proverbs sighs, “Many men claim to be men of unfailing love, but who can find a faithful man?” (Proverbs 20:6). It’s easy to say how much we love our family; being faithful day after day is rare. The good news is that faithfulness doesn’t need to wait for a big occasion. There are many small opportunities today: a phone with nothing to hide, spending with nothing concealed, promises written down. Every small act of faithfulness is a coin dropped into the piggy bank of trust in our marriage.
49	In what small things have I not been faithful enough or clear enough with my spouse?
50	Do I have some “little secrets” that, while not big, keep me from being fully open?
51	In what small thing do you feel I’m very reliable?
52	Is there a small area in our home (time, money, phones, chores) where we could agree to be a little more open with each other?
53	Today, do one small thing you told your spouse you’d do, on time
54	Tell your spouse about a recent purchase, without being asked
55	When you reply to your spouse’s messages, don’t put it off or give a careless answer
56	“He who is faithful in a very little is faithful also in much” — Lord, right now let me be faithful to you in the small things at hand.
57	Trust Can Be Rebuilt
58	Broken trust is slowly repaired through sincere repentance and time
59	If your marriage has ever been through a breach of trust, large or small, you don’t need to rush to revisit the details right now. Simply say quietly to the Lord: “Lord, you know our wounds. Please be our healer.”
60	321 Light: After doing wrong, the self-ruled life wants to turn the page fast: “I already apologized—what more do you want?” That is still self on the throne, demanding that the other person heal on my timetable. Self-emptied repentance hands control to God, humbly accepts the process, and slowly proves through actions that life has truly changed.
61	Peter denied the Lord three times. After the resurrection, Jesus neither pretended nothing had happened nor shamed him. Instead he asked him three times, “Do you love me?” and each question rebuilt a little more. Jesus shows us that restoration needs honesty, and it also needs grace and time; he does not give up on those who fail.
62	Merciful Lord, you heal the brokenhearted and bind up their wounds. Wherever we have hidden things from each other or hurt each other, lead us to confess honestly and turn away sincerely. Give comfort and protection to the one who was hurt, and humility and perseverance to the one who did wrong. Rebuild the trust between us with your own hands. Amen.
63	Trust is like a mirror: once it’s shattered, even if you piece it back together, the cracks remain. Breaches of trust in marriage come in all sizes: a hidden purchase, a string of lies, a relationship with someone of the opposite sex that crossed the line. The hurt spouse often says, “I can forgive you, but I don’t know if I can trust you again.” That statement is honest, not unspiritual.
64	Proverbs says, “He who conceals his sins doesn’t prosper, but whoever confesses and renounces them finds mercy” (Proverbs 28:13). The first step in rebuilding trust is for the one who broke it to confess fully—without downplaying it or shifting blame onto the other—and to truly turn away from it. John the Baptist said, “produce fruits worthy of repentance” (Luke 3:8). Trust is not restored by one “I’m sorry,” but by consistent actions over a long time: being open without being asked, accepting accountability, and waiting patiently.
65	We must also say this gently but clearly: forgiveness means laying down our right to revenge before God, and it can be a decision; trust, though, takes time to rebuild and doesn’t have to happen overnight. If the hurt is deep, please seek professional help from a pastor and a marriage counselor. If there is violence, threats, or control, safety comes first—forgiveness does not mean staying in danger. God can heal, and he often heals through the people he sends.
66	Is there anything I am still hiding that I need to confess before God?
67	When my spouse does wrong, do I demand that they change in my way and at my pace?
68	Is there something between us where you’d like me to be more open, so you can feel more secure? Please say it gently.
69	If we’ve ever had a small crack in our trust, what do you think helped it slowly mend? If your spouse can’t join you right now, write down what you’d like to say to them.
70	Without being asked, tell your spouse about something you’ve seldom explained before
71	If there is something you need to confess, first write it down before God
72	For couples who need help, find a trustworthy pastor or counselor ahead of time
73	“whoever confesses and renounces them finds mercy” — Lord, right now I want to be honest before you and stop hiding.
74	He Who Calls You Is Faithful
75	We may be unfaithful, but he will do it
76	Looking back over this week, you may have seen many places where you weren’t faithful enough. Don’t let condemnation weigh you down. Quietly tell the Lord: “Lord, I can be unfaithful, but you are still faithful. Today I give our marriage to you.”
77	321 Light: Keeping covenant in our own strength eventually leads either to pride (“I’m more faithful than you”) or to despair (“I can’t do it”). The Holy Spirit is my guide: only as the faithful God works within us can we keep our covenant self-emptied, and give all the glory to him.
78	Before he ascended, Jesus promised, “Behold, I am with you always, even to the end of the age” (Matthew 28:20). The disciples had been weak and had run away, but Jesus’ presence was not a reward for their performance; it was his faithful promise. Jesus shows us that faithfulness means not leaving.
79	Faithful Father, you called us into marriage, and you will complete your work in us. Even when we are faithless, you remain faithful. Be the anchor of our marriage; catch us when we slip, and keep us faithful to you and to each other all our lives, until the day the Lord Jesus returns. Amen.
80	In mountain climbing, the other end of the rope is tied to a solid anchor, so the climber dares to keep going up. Even if a foot slips, they won’t fall. Faithfulness in marriage needs an anchor like that. Paul says, “If we are faithless, he remains faithful; for he can’t deny himself” (2 Timothy 2:13). Our faithfulness will slip, but the anchor is God himself.
81	Paul blesses the believers: “May the God of peace himself sanctify you completely,” and then says, “He who calls you is faithful, who will also do it” (1 Thessalonians 5:23–24). It was God who called us into this marriage, and it is God who takes responsibility for shaping us to be more like Christ. Our part is to cooperate; his promise is to do it. That means we don’t have to grit our teeth and hold on in our own strength.
82	This week we have seen that faithfulness comes from God, shows itself in doing what we say and being faithful in small things, and includes repair after a breach. Finally, remember that faithfulness is not perfection; it is returning to the covenant even after failure. A faithful couple is not one that never falls, but one that, having fallen, comes back together before the faithful God and says once more, “I do.”
